This paper examines variation in children's mental health prescribing across the
United States using insurance claims data covering 8 million privately insured
children. I isolate three sources of variation: provider prescribing patterns,
regional practice contexts, and child-level health factors. I find that
eliminating differences in primary care providers' prescribing intensities would
reduce the variance of provider prescribing rates by 50 percent for ADHD
medication and 65 percent for antidepressants. The results also suggest that
higher-quality primary care providers are associated with elevated ADHD
prescribing but lower antidepressant prescribing.
